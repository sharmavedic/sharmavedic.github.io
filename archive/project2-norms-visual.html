How the simulation works

Both pastures use exactly the grazing model from The Problem above — the same cows, the same eat-or-move rule, the same neighbour-seeded regrowth. They start from the same grass, run on the same random seed, and get the same weather. The only thing that differs is the norm.

The state

The weather takes one of four states — — drawn fresh every steps (about  s at normal speed), each equally likely and independent of everything else. The season multiplies how fast grass grows back, by respectively. Nothing else about the pasture changes.

The two norms

A norm maps the state to an action: how many cows each of the four shepherds turns out. The fixed norm answers in every state. The adaptive norm answers across the four seasons. Shepherds re-decide only at a season boundary; destocking sheds the weakest animals first.

Why those numbers

Holding each season fixed and sweeping herd sizes gives a clean optimum per season — one cow in drought, two in dry, three in fair, four in wet — and overstocking costs far more than understocking. Sweeping all 256 possible season-to-herd maps confirms this one is the best of them.

What is being counted

The chart tracks cumulative grass eaten. A cow eats almost exactly what it burns ( of a grass level per step), so this number is really cow-steps sustained — how much grazing the pasture actually supported. It rewards keeping animals alive and fed, not merely owning them.

The controls

Play and pause sit over the pastures, fast forward in the top-right corner and restart in the lower-right; both appear on hover. Restarting deals fresh weather and leaves the run paused. A run lasts steps and then stops on its verdict.
